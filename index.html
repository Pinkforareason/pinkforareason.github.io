Pink for a Reason

I'm Shruthi, and I started this project because I believe awareness is something everyone can help spread — even someone my age. This is the story of why, what I've learned, and how my school raised real support for real research.

Why I Started This

Last year, I began volunteering at a senior care facility near my school, where I would sit and talk with the residents, read to them, and just listen to their stories. A few of them had lost people close to them to breast cancer, and hearing what that loss meant to them stayed with me.

I've always known I wanted to work in medicine — I hope to become a neurosurgeon one day — but that conversation was the first time I understood that helping people doesn't have to wait until you're a doctor. It can start with something as simple as making sure the people around you know the facts, and know that someone their age cares enough to talk about it.

So I decided to build something that could actually do that: a small website, written in my own words, that explains what I've learned and shares what my school did to help.

The Facts Worth Knowing

I'm not a doctor yet, so everything here comes from public health awareness sources, not medical advice. But these are the facts that changed how seriously I took this topic.

1 in 8
women are estimated to develop breast cancer in their lifetime, according to global public health awareness campaigns — which means almost everyone knows someone it could affect.

Early detection is the single fact that comes up again and again in every awareness campaign I researched. When breast cancer is found early, treatment tends to be far more effective — which is exactly why awareness months, self-check reminders, and regular screenings exist: not to alarm people, but to catch things sooner.

October is recognised internationally as Breast Cancer Awareness Month — which is part of why I chose to run my fundraiser during that period, so the message and the giving happened at the same time.

The other fact that stayed with me: awareness campaigns work best when they come from ordinary people talking to people they know, not just posters in a hospital. That's the entire reason this website exists.

My Campaign: Let's Go Pink

In support of the Al Jalila Foundation's medical research, I ran a crowdfunding campaign called "Let's Go Pink for Breast Cancer" through YallaGive. My goal was simple: turn awareness into something that could actually fund research, not just conversation.

AED 2,100+
raised from 40 individual donors, exceeding my original target.
Step 1

Choosing the cause

I chose the Al Jalila Foundation because their work funds real medical research happening right here in the UAE, not just international campaigns I couldn't see the impact of.

Step 2

Setting a target

I set a fundraising goal that felt ambitious but honest, based on what I thought I could realistically ask people close to me to give.

Step 3

Reaching out, one person at a time

I didn't just post a link and hope. I spoke to family, teachers, and family friends directly, explaining why the cause mattered to me personally.

Step 4

Exceeding the goal

Forty people said yes. That number still means more to me than the total amount — forty separate people who decided this was worth their time and money because I asked.

What You Can Do

You don't need a campaign or a website to make a difference. A few small habits, repeated by enough people, add up to real awareness.

  • Talk to the women in your life about the importance of regular self-checks and screenings — awkward conversations save lives.
  • Share one real fact with someone this week, not just a pink ribbon graphic.
  • If you can, support a verified medical research fund like the Al Jalila Foundation, even in a small way.
  • Remember that awareness isn't a one-month event — the facts matter in March just as much as they do in October.

Want to support real research?

My campaign may be complete, but the Al Jalila Foundation's work continues year-round. A small donation goes further than you'd think.
